Soil profile non-NASA — ISRIC SoilGrids

—
Durian wants a deep, friable, well-drained loam at pH 5.5–6.5. Heavy clay holds water against the roots and is the classic Phytophthora setup.

Terrain cross-section

Elevation sampled on a 3×3 grid at ~90 m spacing through your point (west→east and south→north).

Why slope matters more than any weather number

Durian is killed far more often by Phytophthora palmivora in waterlogged root zones than by drought or heat. Root-zone wetness from MERRA-2 is a ~50 km model average — it cannot see your field. Slope can: below about 2% water sits, above about 25% you get erosion and machinery problems. The sweet spot is roughly 3–15%, which is why traditional Malaysian durian orchards sit on hillsides rather than valley floors.

What this app cannot see

Rainfall and soil moisture are ~50 km model grids. Tropical convective rain is patchy — your farm can be dry while the pixel reports 8 mm. A physical rain gauge on the plot beats this app every time; use the app for the regional picture and trends.

NDVI is 250 m. One pixel spans many trees. It sees canopy-scale change, not an individual sick tree.

Nothing here knows your trees. Age, cultivar, rootstock, prior fruiting load, nutrition and pest history drive yield at least as much as climate.
